Bachelor, Master and PhD projects

I supervise Bachelor's and Master's theses, internships and PhD students at the Università di Torino and at HES-SO Valais-Wallis. Projects are linked to my research and often to ongoing funded projects with European partners.

Bachelor projects

Bachelor's theses and final internships for students of Computer Science at the Università di Torino and of Business Information Technology at HES-SO Valais-Wallis. A Bachelor project typically focuses on implementation, simulation or measurement, with a clear and achievable goal for the duration of the thesis.

  • Carbon- and cost-aware energy accounting for edge data centres, with an industrial partner of UNITY-6GUniversità di Torino · internship and thesis, starting

Open topics

Road-ice monitoring with sensors on buses

Collect and analyse road-surface data from sensors on public buses over LoRaWAN or NB-IoT, as a basis for winter road-safety services.

Gossip learning on real devices

Implement serverless distributed learning on phones or Raspberry Pis and measure accuracy, energy and traffic.

Opportunistic information sharing for emergencies

Build or evaluate an app that shares information between nearby devices when the network is down.

Master projects

Master's theses and semester projects for students of the Master's degree in Computer Science at the Università di Torino and for students from partner universities. A Master project tackles an open research question, combining a model or an algorithm with its evaluation; strong theses often lead to a publication at an international conference.

Ongoing

  • Moving base stations in a realistic urban scenario (SUMO, Luxembourg LuST traffic model)Università di Torino · thesis co-supervised with TU Berlin

Open topics

Age of information in IoT networks

Mean-field models of how fresh the information spread by gossip is in large IoT deployments.

Handovers at scale

Mean-field approximations to compute handover rates and their cost in dense, mobile networks.

Performance guarantees for distributed learning

Use network calculus to bound the delay and convergence of distributed and federated learning algorithms.

Communication-efficient federated and gossip learning

Compress models and updates to cut traffic and energy while keeping accuracy.

LEO satellites for emergency communications

Stochastic geometry models of low-Earth-orbit satellite networks to choose energy-optimal transmit powers.

Wireless power for vehicles and IoT

Assess wireless power transfer to battery-less sensors and vehicles: coverage, energy and cost.

Economics of edge computing

Cost models to decide where and when operators should deploy edge (MEC) resources.

PhD projects

PhD positions are funded through research projects at the Università di Torino and at HES-SO Valais-Wallis, often jointly with partner universities. Candidates should hold (or be about to obtain) a Master's degree in computer science, electrical or telecommunications engineering, or a related field, with a solid background in networking, probability or machine learning. Open positions are announced on this site and on LinkedIn; spontaneous applications from strong candidates are welcome.

How to apply

Write to me by email with your CV, your transcript of records, the level of project you are looking for (Bachelor, Master or PhD) and a few lines on the topic or kind of work that interests you. Open topics are starting points: we shape the final project together. Useful background: computer networks, probability, and programming in Python or C/C++.
